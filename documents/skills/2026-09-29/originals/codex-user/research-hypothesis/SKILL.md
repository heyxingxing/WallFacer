# Research Hypothesis

Generate hypotheses from a verified problem, not from novelty theater. Model ratings are advisory; evidence and falsifiability decide what survives.

## Ground the problem

Before proposing solutions, record:

- observed failure or unmet need;
- exact setting and affected users or systems;
- evidence that the problem exists;
- current approaches and why they fail under these conditions;
- constraints on data, compute, time, safety, and evaluation.

If the problem evidence is weak, design a problem-validation study before a method study.

## Generate candidates

Use several operations, keeping the mechanism explicit:

- challenge a hidden assumption;
- compose or decompose existing mechanisms;
- transfer a mechanism from an adjacent domain;
- invert the objective or observation direction;
- analyze limiting cases and failure boundaries.

For each candidate, state:

1. core hypothesis;
2. causal mechanism;
3. observable prediction that differs from alternatives;
4. anti-claim: what result would count against it;
5. minimum falsification experiment;
6. likely code or data lever;
7. novelty, feasibility, and safety uncertainty.

## Select without overclaiming

Rank candidates by problem importance, diagnosticity of the minimum test, feasibility, differentiation from prior work, and downside risk. Do not use a single opaque score. Run a live novelty check before claiming novelty; absence from a quick search is not proof of novelty.

## Deliverable

Produce a hypothesis ledger and recommend no more than 1-3 candidates for the next stage. For each recommendation, include the cheapest decisive test, expected outcomes under the hypothesis and anti-claim, required artifacts, and a stop condition.
